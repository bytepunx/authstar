# D-0019 T-0038 is blocked: the harness cannot serve a tenant's JWKS in-cluster. Split criterion 3's harness half: A

The operator chose answer A on TH-0021: the integration harness's half of S-0009's criterion 3, reading chronicle's report with a genuine portcullis-forwarded ES256 token, split off into its own story under E-0003, S-0017 "Golden path reads chronicle's report with a portcullis-forwarded ES256 token". S-0009 kept only the half its behavior and e2e tests prove with ES256 test tokens.

The reason (I-0007): chronicle fetches `https://authstar.<domain>/.well-known/jwks.json`, with the scheme fixed in `@bytepunx/authstar-fastify`, but portcullis listens plain HTTP on 3710, nothing in `integration/` terminates TLS, no in-cluster DNS maps `authstar.<domain>`, and step 1 creates `acme` without a domain. Fixing it needs a TLS front with a self-signed cert, a CoreDNS rewrite, CA trust in chronicle, and script and config changes, about a story of work that only a live cluster proves. The rejected answers were B, widen S-0009 to do it untested, and C, leave criterion 3 unticked and step 6 failing.

What S-0009 did on that answer: it cancelled T-0038, and T-0086 made golden-path step 6 check only that chronicle refuses an unauthenticated report (401) and skip the token read until S-0017; `integration/README.md` says so. The operator answered at 2026-11-06T18:19:02Z: "A".

S-0017, accepted 2026-11-14, closed I-0007, without the CoreDNS rewrite. Its narrative: "TLS front is a sidecar in chronicle's pod with hostAliases, not a CoreDNS rewrite plus a shared front: story namespaces share one kluster, a cluster-wide rewrite maps the host to one namespace only, and editing kube-system's CoreDNS is shared infrastructure." The sidecar runs chronicle's own image with a Node proxy script from a ConfigMap, "not nginx: no new image or dependency", and a pod sysctl lets the non-root `node` user bind 443. Each deploy loads a harness CA and cert as Secret `authstar-tls`. Golden-path step 1 gives `acme` its domain, and step 6 reads the report through portcullis. `integration/README.md` § "The tenant's JWKS host in-cluster" lists every piece. The cost S-0017 accepted: keep, herald, and web each need their own sidecar, `hostAliases`, and `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS` before they can verify a forwarded token in-cluster (I-0006). D-0041 and D-0042 hold the tower and portcullis fixes it also needed.

S-0006, accepted 2026-11-14, gave keep its share, by the operator's instruction to put keep's harness wiring in S-0006 rather than S-0017. Its narrative: "Keep's tls-front sidecar runs the script from chronicle's ConfigMap `chronicle-tls-front` rather than a copy; the deploy applies every manifest before it waits for a rollout." `integration/manifests/11-keep.yaml` gives keep's pod chronicle's `hostAliases`, the `tls-front` sidecar, and the harness CA through `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`. `integration/config/authstar/keep.yaml` points `towerBaseUrl` at https, with `NODE_TLS_REJECT_UNAUTHORIZED=0` for the call to tower as chronicle has it (I-0001). Herald and web still need the same before they verify a forwarded token in-cluster; reuse the ConfigMap the same way.

S-0015, accepted 2026-11-15, removed that workaround. Its narrative: "Added T-0171 (chronicle's copy) and T-0172 (harness manifests for keep and chronicle): I-0001's second instance names chronicle in the chart and integration/manifests/14-chronicle.yaml, and 11-keep.yaml has had the same workaround since S-0006 (D-0019)." Keep and chronicle now verify tower's SVID on their https `towerBaseUrl` themselves (ADR-0113), and no harness manifest sets `NODE_TLS_REJECT_UNAUTHORIZED`. Herald's harness bundle still points `towerBaseUrl` at http, so herald calls tower with plain fetch there: "Herald's harness bundle uses http:// for tower, so its path is plain fetch there."
